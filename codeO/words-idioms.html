Mistakes to Avoid
  • Prepositions (At / On / In) - Use "at" for specific times ("at 7:30 PM") and specific locations ("at the restaurant"). Use "on" for days/dates ("on Sunday") and surfaces ("on the chair"). Use "in" for months/cities/countries ("in August", "in London", "in Germany").
  • Silent "B" Rule - In words ending with "mb", the "b" is usually silent: Plumber [plum-er], Climb [clime], Dumb [dum], Comb [coam].
  • Left vs. Forgot - Use "left" when the specific location is mentioned ("I left my phone in my room"). Use "forgot" when the location is unknown ("I forgot where I kept my phone").
  • Lineage Query - Asking someone about their birth order among siblings. Formal phrasing: "What is your position in the line of succession as a lineal descendant?" Informal/Funny: "What's your sibling status?"
Sounds & Word Relationships
  • Homograph - Same spelling + different pronunciation/meaning (e.g., lead [to guide] vs. lead [metal]).
  • Homonym - Same sound + same spelling, but different meanings (e.g., bear [animal] vs. bear [to carry]).
  • Homophone - Same sound + different spelling and meaning (e.g., to / too / two).
Initialism vs Acronyms vs Abbreviation
  • Initialism - Abbreviation formed from initial letters where EACH LETTER IS PRONOUNCED INDIVIDUALLY (e.g., AKA, FAQ, HTML, FBI, CEO).
  • Acronym - Abbreviation formed from initial letters that is PRONOUNCED AS A SINGLE WORD (e.g., NASA [nah-sah], ISRO [is-roh], STEM, SaaS).
  • Abbreviation - Shortened form of a word or phrase, often written in shorthand but pronounced as the full word (e.g., Mr. pronounced as Mister, Hr. pronounced as Hour, Dr. pronounced as Doctor).
Idioms & Phrases Overview
  • Phrases - A collection of words used together that gives a literal (actual) meaning.
  • Idioms - A collection of words used together that gives a figurative or different meaning.
