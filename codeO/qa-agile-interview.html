Corporate Laws & Mental Models
  • Gilbert's Law - The biggest workplace challenge is that no one tells you exactly what to do; defining the "Why" before the "How" is your responsibility.
  • Murphy's Law - Anything that can go wrong, will go wrong.
  • Wilson’s Law - When you prioritize knowledge and intelligence in every situation, financial rewards naturally follow.
  • Kidlin’s Law - If you can write out a problem clearly on paper, it is already half solved.
  • Falkland’s Law - When you do not have to make a decision, the best decision is often to make no decision at all.
  • Hick's Law - Limiting choice options allows users to make decisions significantly faster.
  • Miller's Law - The average human memory can hold roughly 7 (± 2) items at a time, which shapes optimal UI item limits.
  • Pareto Principle - 80% of total outcomes result from 20% of core inputs or efforts.
  • Feynman Technique - Learn a concept by explaining it simply to a beginner, identifying gaps, and refining until effortless.
  • Prisoner's Dilemma - A standard game theory model showing why two parties might not cooperate, even when it is in their best interest to do so.
  • Pickle Jar Theory - Prioritize major, high-impact tasks (large rocks) before filling your schedule with small distractions (sand).
  • Red Car Theory - Once your mind tunes into a specific focus (like a red car), you begin noticing opportunities everywhere.
  • Rubber Duck Debugging - Solving complex technical or logic problems by explaining the code line-by-line to an inanimate object.
  • Dead Horse Theory - Stop investing resources into a failed strategy; pivot when progress has halted completely.
  • ECS Model - Exclusiveness, Creativity, and Sales drive corporate value expectations.
  • Nash Equilibrium - Competing businesses naturally cluster together in space or strategy to maximize shared user traffic.
  • Tactics vs. Strategy - Strategy defines "Why" (goals/vision); Tactics define "How" (execution/tools). Strategy must always precede tactics.
  • Culture Eats Strategy for Breakfast - An outstanding strategic plan will fail if the organization's workplace culture is broken (Peter Drucker).

3-3-3 Grounding Rule for Workplace Anxiety
  • The Anxiety Reaction - Under high stress, the brain enters fight-or-flight mode, causing focus to drop as the prefrontal cortex goes offline.
  • Step 1 (Listen) - Close your eyes for 1 minute and focus calmly on 3 distinct sounds around you.
  • Step 2 (Observe) - Open your eyes and visually examine 3 physical objects on your desk, noting their colors and shapes.
  • Step 3 (Touch) - Physically touch those 3 objects to feel their texture, weight, and temperature.
  • Biological Outcome - This grounding exercise shifts your nervous system from sympathetic (stress) to parasympathetic (rest and digest), restoring focus and composure.
Interview Timing & Negotiation Strategy
  • Best Months - January to March (Good) and April to June (Best, right after annual hikes).
  • Moderate Months - July to September (Decent to try).
  • Worst Months - October to December (Typically worst, especially for 10+ years experience due to holiday freezes).
  • Fiscal Year Advantage - The first half of the fiscal year is generally the best time for job hunting.
  • Competency Over Timing - When you have high competency and self-confidence, the hiring month matters less.
  • Interview Conversation Flow - Never abruptly end a conversation during an interview; keep it professional and open.
  • Indirect Time Delay - Politely stall for time during offers using indirect reasons (e.g., "I want to discuss this with my family/wife first").
  • Negotiation Rule - Always negotiate before signing, but once the official Offer Letter (OL) is released and accepted, do not renegotiate—it is unprofessional.
  • Win-Win Principle - Negotiations should always aim for a win-win situation for both parties.
  • Alternative Plans - Always have a fallback option or alternative plan ready when entering a negotiation.

Inside the Office & Leadership Dynamics
  • Attrition Benchmarks - Below 10% is excellent, 10–20% is acceptable, and anything above 20% is a major red flag.
  • Retention Strategy - High retention and a low attrition rate are symbols of a peaceful, healthy work culture.
  • Leadership Words - CEO and CTO words carry immense power, reflecting company values and reputation.
  • Constructive Framing - Address current issues (e.g., company or project challenges) in a positive, proactive manner—take responsibility to help clean it up rather than just pointing fingers.
  • Start with Why - Always anchor thinking, speaking, and acting starting with "Why" (the purpose), then "How" (uniqueness), and finally "What" (product/service).
  • Professional Communication - Avoid imperative words like "must" in an office setting as they sound too forceful.

KPI vs. KRA
  • KRA (Key Result Area) - Defined by the company; represents your core job role and assigned organizational targets (e.g., hiring 30 people).
  • KPI (Key Performance Indicator) - Employee's plan and productivity metrics used to measure performance (e.g., how much revenue each hire generates for the organization). KRA comes first, followed by KPI.
Proper Office Exit Procedure
  • Steps to Leave - Collect final salary and slips from HR -> Secure an offer letter with a confirmed Date of Joining (DOJ) from your next employer -> Draft resignation email -> Inform your manager first, then HR -> Negotiate your last working day professionally.

Human Biology, Health & Metabolism
  • Science & Medicine - Medicine is a practical branch of science; all medicine is science, but not all science is medicine.
  • NEET - National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (entrance exam for medical courses in India).
  • Digestive Tract Length - Human digestive tract length is approximately 9 times the length of the human torso.
  • Dopamine & Homeostasis - Dopamine is a neurotransmitter driving motivation/reward. Homeostasis is the body's self-regulating mechanism to maintain stable internal states (like 37°C temperature).

  • Serotonin - Key neurotransmitter and hormone regulating sleep, mood, and digestion.
  • Caloric Value of Fat - 1 kg of stored body fat equals ~7,700 kcal.
  • Daily Caloric Needs - Average human maintenance intake is 2,000–2,500 kcal/day.
  • BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) - Calories burned at complete rest to maintain vital functions. Harris-Benedict formula (Men): (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) - (5 × age) + 5.
  • TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) - Total calories burned per day = BMR + Exercise + TEF (Digestion). Active multiplier is ~1.75 × BMR.
  • Placebo Effect - Psychological and physical healing response triggered by belief in a treatment despite receiving an inactive substance.
  • Breakfast Window - Consume breakfast within 1–2 hours of waking (e.g., before 8 AM if waking at 6 AM) to balance blood sugar and metabolism.

First Aid & CPR Protocols
  • CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) - 30 chest compressions to 2 rescue breaths. Use two hands for adults, one hand for children, and two fingers for infants.
  • Cardiac Arrest vs. Heart Attack - Cardiac arrest is an electrical rhythm failure; heart attack is a circulatory/vascular blockage. Calf muscles act as a secondary venous pump returning blood to the heart.
  • Wound Care - Sterile gauze acts as the primary absorbable layer; Micropore tape secures dressing; Betadine (povidone-iodine) prevents bacterial infection on cuts/burns.

  • Demat Account - Short for Dematerialised Account; an electronic account holding securities like shares, bonds, and mutual funds digitally (similar to how a bank account holds cash).
  • Multiple Demat Accounts - An individual can open multiple Demat accounts, but maintaining too many can lower net profits due to recurring annual maintenance charges across accounts.
  • Government Spending & Taxes - High direct taxation is only part of the equation; government spending is the true tax. There is no unbalanced budget—deficits are paid either through direct taxes, inflation, or debt with interest.
PAN vs TAN
  • PAN (Permanent Account Number) - Used for opening bank accounts and filing personal income tax returns; carries no expiry date.
  • TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) - Required for businesses and organizations filing TDS/TCS returns; valid for one financial year and requires annual renewal.
Corporate Accounting & Salary Definitions
  • Gross CTC - Total Cost to Company before any deductions.
  • Net CTC - Actual in-hand salary received after all statutory and tax deductions.
  • Zero-Based Budgeting - A budgeting method where every expense must be justified from a zero baseline for each new period, helping companies eliminate trivial spending and focus on non-trivial priorities.
  • EBITDA - Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization; a key metric showing core operational profitability before non-operating expenses.
Indian Taxation & Income Tax Terms
  • Tax Liability - The total legal tax obligation owed by an earner to the government for a given financial year.
  • TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) - Tax collected at the point of income generation by the deductor.
  • Section 80C - Provision under the Income Tax Act allowing tax deductions up to ₹1.5 Lakh for investments in eligible instruments (e.g., PPF, ELSS, Insurance, FDs/RDs).
  • Section 87A - Tax rebate offering relief (up to ₹25,000 under the New Tax Regime) to zero out tax liability for eligible resident individuals.
  • Standard Deduction & Rebates - Income levels up to ₹12L–₹12.75L benefit from standard deductions and rebates ensuring zero effective tax liability. Earnings exceeding ₹13L fall into progressive tax slabs.
  • Marginal Relief - Tax relief granted when income slightly exceeds a threshold slab, preventing the tax increase from exceeding the extra income earned.
  • Form 16 - Annual TDS certificate issued by employers detailing salary earned and tax deducted.
  • Form 26AS - Consolidated annual tax credit statement on the Income Tax e-filing portal reflecting all TDS, advance tax, and self-assessment tax credited to your PAN.
  • ITR-1 (Sahaj) - Income tax return form for resident individuals with income up to ₹50 Lakhs from salary, one house property, and other sources.
  • ITR-6 - Income tax return form for companies other than those claiming exemption under Section 11.

Sales Dynamics & Strategy
  • Emotional Selling - Add emotional resonance to products when selling rather than pushing hard sales. If a representative or client displays disrespect, escalate officially.
  • Rejection in Sales - Expecting 100 "No"s is the cost of securing one "Yes"; cold calling averages around a 2% success rate.
  • Product Choice Paradox - Offering excessive product varieties reduces overall sales, whereas offering a curated/limited line drives higher conversion (e.g., Apple model).
  • Customer Retention Value - The cost of losing a sale three times equals the long-term impact of losing one loyal customer.
